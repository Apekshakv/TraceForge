Leaks are easy to miss, hard to prove

A confidential screenshot shows up on a public forum. Who leaked it? Is the image carrying something else inside? Can you prove your evidence was not touched?

01

Who leaked it?

Insiders share screenshots and documents. Tracing a public copy to a recipient is guesswork.

02

Is an image hiding something?

Attackers hide commands and stolen data inside normal-looking images (MITRE ATT&CK T1027.003, T1567).

03

Can we prove it?

Insurers, auditors and courts need evidence that has not been altered.

Today, those are three different tools. TraceForge is one workflow.

How it works

Six steps from a shared file to a verified finding.

  1. Protect

    A unique recipient ID is embedded invisibly in every shared copy.

  2. Collect

    An OSINT collector scans permitted public sources for files tied to the organisation.

  3. Analyse

    Extract the watermark, score images for hidden payloads, scan metadata.

  4. Explain

    An LLM writes the report, maps MITRE ATT&CK and assigns a risk level.

  5. Preserve

    Hash with SHA-256, sign with Ed25519, append to a hash-chained log.

  6. Verify

    The verifier returns match or mismatch for any evidence file.

Every finding ends as Critical Suspicious or Clean.

Every finding passes through analysis, explanation and a signed evidence log

A leaked copy found by the OSINT Collector is checked three ways in parallel, explained by the LLM, then signed into the evidence log before it reaches the dashboard.
